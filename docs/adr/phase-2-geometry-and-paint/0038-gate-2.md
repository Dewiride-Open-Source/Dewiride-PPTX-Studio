## Writing the claim down turned it into a measurement

The gate's nine features had never been anything but a sentence in the plan. Written out as
detections over committed markup — `tools/gate2/features.ts` — the sentence became checkable, and
two of the nine failed immediately. Across all 155 committed slides:

| Gate 2 names              | slides, before | slides, now |
| ------------------------- | -------------: | ----------: |
| Gradient fills            |              4 |           5 |
| Pattern fills             |              2 |           2 |
| Cropped image fills       |              2 |           2 |
| Dashed strokes            |              3 |           3 |
| Arrowheads                |              2 |           2 |
| Shadows                   |              3 |           3 |
| Glows                     |              3 |           3 |
| **Nested rotated groups** |          **0** |       **1** |
| **Themed backgrounds**    |          **0** |       **3** |

Both zeros were real, and the second was hidden behind a mistake worth naming: the first scan looked
for `a:bgRef`. **There is no `a:bgRef`.** The element is `p:bgRef`, in PresentationML, and searching
for the DrawingML spelling reads zero across fifty-two decks while every one of them has a
background. The same trap `a03-fills` documents for `blipFill`, one namespace over. The gate now
tests `p:bgRef`, and an adversarial case asserts the `a:` spelling matches nothing.

Corrected, the finding stood anyway: **every corpus background was the same one** —
`<p:bgRef idx="1001"><a:schemeClr val="bg1"/></p:bgRef>`, on the master, on all fifty-two. No slide
and no layout overrode it, no other `@idx` appeared, and no `p:bgPr` existed anywhere.

The transform finding was starker. **No committed slide carried a non-zero `@rot` or any `@flipH` or
`@flipV`, on any shape, anywhere.** Group nesting reached depth 1 and no further. Sub-phase 2.10's
whole subject — flip composing innermost, and a child placed by
`off + (x − chOff) × (ext / chExt)` — was measured through PowerPoint's object model in experiment
C6 and unit-tested against that fixture, and no corpus slide had ever exercised it against a pixel.

## The two decks

`a44-transforms` (2 slides) and `a45-backgrounds` (4 slides), Tier A, generated. Both needed the
chassis to grow: `GroupSpec` had no way to write `@rot`/`@flipH`/`@flipV` on a group's own `a:xfrm`,
and `ProbeSlide`/`ProbeLayout` had nowhere to put a `p:bg` — which has to be the **first** child of
`p:cSld`, before `p:spTree`.

**a44** is twelve transforms on an `rtTriangle`, chosen because it is the cheapest shape asymmetric
in both axes at once, each square and centred in its cell so a 45° rotation stays inside it. The
last two — `flipH rot 30` and `flipV rot 30` — are the pair a renderer composing rotation before the
mirror draws at the wrong angle; the ten before them read the same under either order. Slide 2 is
six groups: the identity, a child space halved so every child draws at twice its written size, a
rotated group, a mirrored one, a rotated group holding a mirrored one, and a child space five
million EMU from the group's own origin.

**a45** separates the 1000-offset so a renderer that ignores it cannot pass by luck: `idx="2"` is
`fillStyleLst[1]`, a **gradient**, while `idx="1002"` is `bgFillStyleLst[1]`, a near-white **solid**.
Reading either list for the other swaps a gradient for a flat tint across the whole slide. Slide 3
is an explicit `p:bgPr`; slide 4 has no `p:bg` at all and inherits its layout's.

### PowerPoint was asked before anything was scored

Opening `a45` through COM and reading `Slide.Background.Fill` back:

| slide | markup           | PowerPoint reports                       |
| ----- | ---------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| 1     | `bgRef idx=2`    | `Type=3` gradient, **2 stops**           |
| 2     | `bgRef idx=1002` | `Type=1` solid, `#EE884F`                |
| 3     | `bgPr` gradient  | `Type=3` gradient, **3 stops**           |
| 4     | none             | `FollowMasterBackground=true`, `#D5E3CF` |

Slide 1 is a gradient and slide 2 is a solid, from indices two apart. That is the 1000-offset,
measured rather than read off 2.9's citation, and slide 4's pale green is `accent6` at `tint 40%` —
the **layout's** background, not the master's.

## The reference is PowerPoint, not LibreOffice

The plan names a LibreOffice reference and this does not use one. That is a deviation, not a
softening: LibreOffice is a second implementation with opinions of its own, and this repository's
first rule is to ask the format's author. 3.9 already commits PowerPoint's answer for every corpus
slide as a reduced grid, so the gate needs no Office installed and runs from a clone.

What LibreOffice would still buy is a reference that runs on a **Linux CI runner**, which PowerPoint
cannot. That is the same gap the carried entry "CI cannot run the fidelity gate" already names, and
it stays open. Installing LibreOffice is not something this session did.

## The heatmap, and what it found in one look

Three panels a slide: PowerPoint, ours, and the difference. The difference is drawn as SVG
rectangles at one per run of equal cells — a cell is the unit the score is computed in, so drawing
cells shows exactly what was compared, and it needs no image encoder in the repository.

The mean did not surface what the picture did. **`a04-effects-03` scored 9591 bp, nowhere near the
worst ten, and its shape was the wrong colour end to end** — 2848 cells in one region. Two findings
came out of looking at it:

**PowerPoint reads `a:effectDag`, though it writes none.** The model's comment said the element "has
never been observed to write", which is true and was being read as "so it does not matter". Asked
through COM, the shape reports `Glow.Radius = 8` and `Shadow.Visible = true`, and its export
composites the glow over the fill while the object model still reports the fill as `accent2`. We
draw the plain fill and no effects. The comment now records the measurement; implementing the DAG is
a sub-phase, not a footnote.

**Two `a:blip` colour effects were emitting a colour 255 times too dark.** `Rgba` channels are 0..1
— it says so on the type — and `image/blip.ts` treated them as 0..255:

```
duotone     slope: (b - a) / 255   →   slope: b - a
clrChange   indicator(Math.round(from.r))   →   indicator(Math.round(from.r * 255))
```

The first renders every duotone image flat black. The second is worse: rounding a 0..1 channel gives
0 or 1, so the 256-entry match table marked near-black as the colour to replace, and the effect fired
on the wrong pixels. Both were **measured correctly in 2.12** — `blips.json` has the rules, 63
probes, 28 unit tests, 8/8 mutants — and both shipped wrong, because every one of those tests
asserted the rule and none evaluated the filter. This is 2.10's gradient failure again, in a
different package: unit tests green, package painting flat blocks, and only a pixel comparison to
notice.

`a03-fills-02`: **9378 → 9638 bp**, maxD 194 → 171. Corpus mean 9756 → 9766.

## Verification

- **Gate 2 holds**: all nine features carried, 14 slides drawn, `pnpm gate2` exits 0.
- 3179 → **3216 tests**, 91 files. The three new render-svg tests **evaluate** the emitted filter
  rather than matching its attribute text, which is exactly what the old ones failed to do.
- **10 of 10 mutants killed**: both unit errors above, the band edges, the heatmap's transparent
  floor, the chroma decimation, the nested-group conjunction, the two negative lookaheads, the
  group's `a:xfrm` attributes, and the background's position inside `p:cSld`.
- The `drawSlide` extraction in `raster/render.ts` is proved behaviour-preserving by the harness
  itself: 146 existing slides rasterised to **byte-identical** digests across the refactor.
- **`pnpm gate2` runs inside `pnpm check`**, which `gate1` cannot: it needs no Office and the
  reference is committed, so the feature table above is re-derived on every run rather than
  trusted.
- `pnpm check` green; round trip **54/54**; fidelity 152 scored, 3 not drawn.

## Open questions

1. **`a:effectDag` is read by PowerPoint and not by us.** Measured here; unimplemented. It needs its
   own sub-phase — the DAG is a full compositing graph over twenty-odd primitives, not a list.
2. **`a04-effects-01` is now the worst slide in the gate set** at 9491 bp, 803 cells at maxD 91. The
   eight `a:effectLst` children are drawn but not exactly; no probe separates the causes yet.
3. **`a09-fields`'s oracle is a function of the capture date.** Re-capturing changed exactly one
   committed grid, and it was the deck with `a:fld` date fields. The score will drift as the
   captured date recedes, and nothing currently says so out loud.
4. **`a03-fills-02` still differs down its right column** — 430 cells at maxD 171 in cells
   `[88,12,115,45]`, which is `blipFill tile flip` above `blip biLevel lum`. Two of the eight
   `a:blip` probes; the duotone and `a:clrChange` beside them were the two fixed here. The gate
   shows them rather than hiding them, which is the whole argument for the page existing.
5. **`a44-transforms-01` is not in the gate's demo set.** Gate 2 names nested rotated _groups_, and
   the detection is that conjunction, so the twelve shape-level transforms are scored by the
   fidelity harness but not shown by the gate. Correct as written, and worth revisiting if the
   gate's wording is ever reopened.
